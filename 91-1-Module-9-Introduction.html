Module 9 Introduction

The second half

Now you use the notation

Module 8 gave you the profession’s notation: debits and credits, the journal entry, the T-account, posting, and the chart of accounts.

This module is the second half of The Analyst’s Tool Kit. You will see what the profession builds with that notation—the moves and account structures that make a period readable.

At the period’s edge

Some entries have no document trigger

Adjusting entries are made at the period’s edge so the chosen period’s picture is right. Then temporary accounts and closing put that period away, readying the system for the next one.

Built for reading

Account structures preserve the useful detail

Contra and adjunct accounts work in pairs with another account. Together they show you more than one net number could.

A control account gives you one line to read, while subsidiary accounts keep the book of names and amounts behind it.

Two loaded names

The plug and the title line

Goodwill is the plug with a name: the amount left when a purchase price exceeds the identified net assets acquired.

Consolidation is what the word on a real statement title line means: the parent and the entities it controls are presented as one reporting entity.

Nothing here is new; only the names are.
