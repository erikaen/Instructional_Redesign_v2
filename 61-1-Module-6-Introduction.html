Module 6 Introduction

The turn

The map, held against an entity nobody owns

Module 5 held the map you built in the bike shop against CVS Health — a $250-billion company owned by shareholders. The map applied. Module 6 holds it against something genuinely different: a nonprofit — an organization that nobody owns. No shares, no shareholders, nobody with a claim on what's left. So here is the question this module turns on: if the right side of the balance sheet was about claims on the difference, what happens to it when no one holds that claim?

The organization

Meet the Oregon Humane Society

Founded in 1868, OHS runs shelters and veterinary hospitals in Portland and Salem, Oregon. In 2025 it placed 12,478 pets with new families — a 97% live-release rate — performed thousands of surgeries in its teaching hospital, enforced the state's animal-protection laws with its own commissioned agents, and reached a hundred thousand people with its education programs. About 29,000 donors funded that work. It is real, it is human-scale — about $41 million of expenses a year, closer to the bike shop than to CVS — and its audited statements are the module's text.

The statement set

Four statements, not three

OHS publishes a Statement of Financial Position (their name for the balance sheet), a Statement of Activities (it plays the income statement's role), a Statement of Cash Flows (same name, same job), and one statement the course has never seen before — a Statement of Functional Expenses: expenses broken down by kind and by function. New names, one new statement, and the same map underneath.

The best way in is to see all four at once.
