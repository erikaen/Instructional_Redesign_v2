## The page, in full (near-student voice — this IS the draft 61-1 body)

**The map, held against an entity nobody owns.**

Module 5 held the map you built in the bike shop against CVS Health — a
$250-billion company owned by shareholders. The map applied. Module 6 holds
it against something genuinely different: a **nonprofit** — an organization
that **nobody owns**. No shares, no shareholders, nobody with a claim on
what's left. So here is the question this module turns on: if the right side
of the balance sheet was about claims on the difference, what happens to it
when no one holds that claim?

**Meet the Oregon Humane Society.**

Founded in 1868, OHS runs shelters and veterinary hospitals in Portland and
Salem, Oregon. In 2025 it placed **12,478 pets** with new families — a 97%
live-release rate — performed thousands of surgeries in its teaching
hospital, enforced the state's animal-protection laws with its own
commissioned agents, and reached a hundred thousand people with its education
programs. About **29,000 donors** funded that work. It is real, it is
human-scale — about $41 million of expenses a year, closer to the bike shop
than to CVS — and its audited statements are the module's text.

**Four statements, not three.**

OHS publishes a *Statement of Financial Position* (their name for the balance
sheet), a *Statement of Activities* (it plays the income statement's role), a
*Statement of Cash Flows* (same name, same job), and one statement the course
has never seen before — a *Statement of Functional Expenses*: expenses broken
down by kind and by function. New names, one new statement, and the same map
underneath.

The best way in is to see all four at once. → **The Statement Map** (62-1).

## Draft learning objectives (OHS edition — for Rick's edit; wired into
Learning-Objectives/ after the module ships)

By the end of Module 6, the student will be able to:

- **M6-LO1:** Identify the accounting entity in OHS's statements and explain
  what does — and does not — enter its records at the boundary (the OCF
  funds excluded for variance power; the volunteers' unrecognized hours).
- **M6-LO2:** Name OHS's four statements, map each onto the course's
  architecture (balance sheet · income-statement role · cash · the
  functional breakdown), and state the formal element definitions: assets —
  economic benefits owned or controlled; liabilities — obligations to
  sacrifice economic benefits in the future; net assets — the difference.
- **M6-LO3:** Explain why the difference is called **Net Assets**, how it is
  classified with / without donor restrictions, and why board designations
  remain Without — only donors create binding restrictions.
- **M6-LO4:** Explain **net assets released from restrictions** as a
  zero-sum transfer between the two piles, and locate it on the Statement
  of Activities.
- **M6-LO5:** Read the Statement of Functional Expenses as one total broken
  down two ways at once — by kind and by function — and compute the
  program-vs-supporting ratio from it.
- **M6-LO6:** Answer "how did OHS do this year?" four ways (operating
  result, change in net assets, operating cash, change in cash), say why
  each frame exists, and use the liquidity footnote to distinguish net
  assets from spendable resources.

## Draft expandable for 63-4: "About the name Net Assets" (near-student voice)

> **A note on the name — click to expand.**
>
> Back in Module 1 you built a tab and named it **Reasons** — the reasons the
> difference between what the shop had and what it owed had moved. You may
> also remember how long we went WITHOUT naming the difference itself. It was
> just "the difference," A − L, for page after page — and that was
> deliberate. Here is why.
>
> The difference has now worn three names in this course. At the shop it
> earned the name **Member's Capital** — one member, one claim. At CVS it was
> called **shareholders' equity** — many owners, tradeable claims. At OHS it
> is called **Net Assets** — because nobody owns the place at all, so no
> claim-word fits. Three names; the thing itself never changed once:
> recorded assets minus recorded liabilities, whatever that difference
> happens to be. The name is chosen to fit who the organization answers to —
> which is exactly why we would not let a name get ahead of the idea.
>
> And the Reasons stayed reasons everywhere. At the shop the piles tracked
> where the difference came from — Contributed, Generated, Withdrawn. At OHS
> the piles track promises instead — without donor restrictions, with donor
> restrictions. Different questions asked of the same difference; a tab named
> Reasons can hold either answer.
>
> One warning, carried over from Module 5: do not let the plain-sounding
> name fool you. "Net Assets" can sound like "the assets, netted down" —
> spendable money. It is not. At OHS it is $107.2 million, and most of it is
> buildings and fenced funds, not a bank balance. How much OHS can actually
> spend is an asset-side question with its own page (63-6).
