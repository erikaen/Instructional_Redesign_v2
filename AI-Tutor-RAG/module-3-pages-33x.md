# Module 3 — Tutorial 3: Build the Income Statement

This RAG knowledge base helps the AI Tutor support students who are lost,
confused, or unsure what to do in the Bike Repair accounting pre-work.

## Tutor conduct reminders

The course teaches by discovery. Ask an orienting question first, then give a
nudge, then a stronger hint only if needed. Do not lead with a placement, total,
or final relationship. Confirm it after a genuine attempt or after the page
reveals it.

Use the page's language:

- describe transactions through what the Company gives and receives;
- Revenue is the value of completed work the Company gave customers between
  chosen dates;
- Expenses are value used up while the Company completed that work;
- describe an amount as recorded or recognized;
- records **show** amounts; the Company **has** assets and obligations;
- use singular `Member's Capital` if that name becomes relevant;
- use “accounting identity” if the Assets-minus-Liabilities framing is needed.

The repair work is an activity, not an entity that can own, owe, choose, agree,
or receive. Documents and June–August are not decision-makers. Name the Company,
a customer, Anna, or the student when an actor is needed.

The Company's records begin at formation on June 1. Do not import personal
activity from before formation. New canon for this page:

- GENERATED dated amount on June 1: **$0**;
- GENERATED dated amount on August 31: **$2,640**;
- checked season rows between those lines: **46**.

The dated lines are boundaries and tie-out amounts, not two additional season
rows. Revenue and Expenses are groupings of copied GENERATED rows. The Reasons
ledger remains in place.

This page has no typed-answer input. Students use buttons, tabs, and two gated
sorters. The header has glossary and restart controls; restart reloads the page.
Continue controls remain gated until the current action is complete.

Sorter rows appear on the left and category destinations on the right. The
student selects a row and destination. Once every representative row is correct,
the page can complete the repeated rows by the same rule. The finished passes
include all 46 copied rows, even though the student does not manually place each
one.

## 33-1-build-the-income-statement.html — Build the Income Statement

### What this page is

Tutorial 3, “Build the Statement,” is one approximately 15-minute page. It turns
the 46 checked GENERATED season rows into a finished Income Statement without
moving or deleting the source rows.

The page's five beats are:

1. Copy the season rows.
2. Sort Revenue from Expenses.
3. Sort the Expenses by kind.
4. Format the statement.
5. Christen the statement and tie it to GENERATED.

The workbook starts on `Reasons · GENERATED`. Assets, Liabilities, Reasons, and
Balance Sheet tabs are visible. The amber Working Tab appears after the copy.
It becomes the purple Income Statement tab at completion.

The final “One question remains” button goes to
`34-1-what-cash-missed.html`.

## Beat 1 — Copy the Season Rows

### What the student sees and does

The phase title is “Copy the Season Rows.” The Reasons tab is open to GENERATED.
The narrative identifies $0 on June 1, $2,640 on August 31, and 46 checked season
rows between the dated lines. Anna calls the rows the raw material for answering
how the shop did.

The student clicks one control:

`Copy the 46 season rows onto a working sheet →`

The page identifies the selected rows, creates an amber Working Tab, copies all
46 rows in one action, opens that tab, and moves into Beat 2. No keyboard copy,
individual row selection, or typing is required.

### Meaning to preserve

This is a copy, not a removal. Reasons keeps the source ledger. The Working Tab
contains copies used for sorting. The two dated lines establish the boundaries
and later tie-out; they are not part of the 46-row count.

## Beat 2 — Sort Revenue from Expenses

### What the student sees and does

The phase title is “Sort Revenue from Expenses.” The narrative says nothing is
set aside: all 46 copied rows must end in Revenue or Expenses.

Anna defines the destinations before sorting:

- Revenue records completed work the Company gave customers during the season,
  whichever way cash moved.
- Expenses record value used up while the Company completed that work.

Representative rows include Dana's collected repair, June rent, Ridgeline's
unpaid completed repair, Smith's delivered frame, and summer depreciation. The
student classifies every shown row. The gate opens only when all shown rows are
correct; the remaining repeated rows then follow the same rule. All 46 copied
rows are included in the final grouping.

The result shows Revenue above Expenses. Revenue totals **$6,020**.

### Recognition cases to preserve

Ridgeline is Revenue although payment has not arrived. The Company gave the
completed repair service during the season and has a right to receive $300
later.

Smith is Revenue although the customer's cash came earlier. At formation, the
Company assumed the customer-deposit claim. During the season, the Company gave
Smith the completed frame and recognized Revenue.

Depreciation is an Expense because it recognizes value used up during the
season. Cash need not move at that moment.

### Common sticking points and hint ladders

**“How do I choose a side?”**

- Orient: “What did the Company give, or what does the row say was used up?”
- Nudge: “Completed repairs and frames concern value given to customers; parts,
  rent, and depreciation concern value used up.”
- Stronger hint: “Put completed-work rows in Revenue and used-up rows in
  Expenses.”

**“Why does unpaid Ridgeline count?”**

- Orient: “Was the repair finished and given to Ridgeline this season?”
- Nudge: “Separate the completed service from the later cash receipt. Which does
  Revenue recognize?”
- Stronger hint: “The completed $300 service is Revenue now; the Company has a
  right to receive $300 later.”

**“Why does Smith count if the cash was earlier?”**

- Orient: “When did the Company give Smith the completed frame?”
- Nudge: “At formation the Company assumed the claim; the season row shows it
  satisfying that claim.”
- Stronger hint: “Smith is Revenue because the Company gave the frame during
  the season, regardless of when the customer paid.”

**“Why is depreciation an Expense without cash?”**

- Orient: “Does an Expense require cash now, or does it record value used up?”
- Nudge: “Read what the depreciation row recognizes during the season.”
- Stronger hint: “It belongs in Expenses because it recognizes using-up without
  requiring cash to move then.”

**“Why can't I continue?”**

- Orient: “Is a representative row unsorted or incorrectly placed?”
- Nudge: “The gate requires every shown row to be correct.”
- Stronger hint: “Recheck Dana, June rent, Ridgeline, Smith, and depreciation;
  then use the offered completion control.”

### Tutor-only answer key — Beat 2

**TUTOR-ONLY.**

- Revenue: all completed repair and frame rows, including Dana, Ridgeline, and
  Smith.
- Expenses: all parts-used, rent-used, and depreciation rows.
- Revenue appears above Expenses and totals **$6,020**.
- Ridgeline counts despite later payment; Smith counts despite earlier payment.
- The sorter groups copies; the GENERATED ledger is unchanged.

## Beat 3 — Sort the Expenses by Kind

### What the student sees and does

Revenue is grouped. The phase title becomes “Sort the Expenses by Kind.” The
student now sorts every Expense row into:

- Parts Used;
- Rent;
- Depreciation.

Representative rows include Dana's parts, June rent, Ridgeline's parts, and
summer depreciation. Anna distinguishes shelf-measured parts, workspace use as
months pass, and wear and aging. She flags Ridgeline: its parts were used on a
completed job even though payment has not arrived.

The gate again requires every shown row to be correct before the repeated rows
are completed. The result is:

- Parts Used: **$1,200**;
- Rent: **$1,950**;
- Depreciation: **$230**.

### Common sticking points and hint ladders

- Nudge: “Leave Revenue grouped; this pass uses only Expense rows.”
- Stronger hint: “Sort each Expense into Parts Used, Rent, or Depreciation.”

**“Where do Ridgeline's parts go?”**

- Nudge: “The unpaid amount affects the Revenue case, not the kind of Expense.”
- Stronger hint: “Put ‘Parts used — Ridgeline Trail Crew’ in Parts Used.”

**“How do I tell Rent from Depreciation?”**

- Stronger hint: “Monthly workspace use goes to Rent; wear and aging go to

**“Why is the next step locked?”**

- Orient: “Are all four representative rows correctly placed?”
- Nudge: “Check Dana's parts, June rent, Ridgeline's parts, and depreciation.”
- Stronger hint: “Correct those four, then use the completion control for the

### Tutor-only answer key — Beat 3

**TUTOR-ONLY.**

- June, July, and August workspace-use rows → Rent, total **$1,950**.

## Beat 4 — Format the Statement

### What the student sees and does

The phase title is “Format the Statement.” The Working Tab shows the finished
groups: Revenue $6,020, Parts Used $1,200, Rent $1,950, and Depreciation $230.
Anna says the sorting is done.

The student clicks:

`Fold and rule the totals →`

The page folds Revenue detail into one line. It also creates one line for each
Expense kind:

- Revenue $6,020;
- Parts Used $1,200;
- Rent $1,950;
- Depreciation $230.

The three detailed depreciation entries fold into one **$230** line, keeping
Anna's promise. The page rules the totals and advances to the christening gate.
No manual cell formatting or typed total is required.

The presentation summarizes detail; it does not erase source rows. Total
expenses are $3,380, and the ruled difference is $6,020 − $3,380 = $2,640.

## Beat 5 — Christen the Statement

### What the student sees and does

The phase title becomes “Christen the Statement.” Anna says the rows are folded,
the totals are ruled, and the report is ready to earn its name. The page shows
Revenue $6,020, Total expenses $3,380, and their $2,640 difference.

The student clicks:

`Rename the Working Tab as Income Statement →`

The page renames Working Tab to **Income Statement**, changes the tab from amber
to purple, and presents:

- Revenue: **$6,020**;
- Total expenses: **$3,380**;
- Net income: **$2,640**.

Anna confirms the three depreciation details appear together as one $230 line.

### The GENERATED tie and cash question

The records show GENERATED at **$0 on June 1** and **$2,640 on August 31**:

`$0 + $2,640 = $2,640`

That $2,640 increase agrees with Net income. The Income Statement summarizes the
46 rows between the dates; Reasons retains those source rows.

The final message also shows Net income **$2,640** and Cash **$620**, down from
**$1,300** on June 1. Both are true because they answer different questions.
Net income compares completed work given to customers with value used up. Cash
shows how much cash the Company has at a date. Cash receipts and payments need
not share the timing of Revenue and Expenses.

The page does not reconcile every cash movement here. “One question remains”
continues to `34-1-what-cash-missed.html`.
